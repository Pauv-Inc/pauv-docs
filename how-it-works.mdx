## The Net Public Sentiment Index

Every public figure on Pauv has an **NPSI**: a live, dollar-denominated measure of their cultural relevance. The value is determined solely by forecasting activity on Pauv. There are no social metrics, no algorithmic feeds, and no external data sources pushed into the index.

- **Floor at zero.** No matter how much negative forecasting happens, the NPSI approaches zero asymptotically but never reaches it.
- **No ceiling.** The NPSI can grow without bound.
- **Not an asset.** Users do not own the NPSI. It is a measure of public perception, not a financial instrument.

When a figure drops a hit, wins a title, or goes viral, users react, forecasts get opened, and the NPSI moves in real time.

## Positive and Negative Forecasts

Users interact with Pauv by opening and closing forecasts. A forecast is a directional view on a public figure's cultural relevance, expressed through the NPSI.

| Forecast Type | What It Means | Effect on NPSI |
|---------------|---------------|----------------|
| **Positive Forecast** | The user believes the figure's cultural relevance will rise. | Pulls the NPSI value **up**. |
| **Negative Forecast** | The user believes the figure's cultural relevance will fall. | Pulls the NPSI value **down**. |

## The Valence Bonding Curve (VBC 4.1)

**VBC 4.1** is Pauv's proprietary pricing engine. Every NPSI is governed by a single continuous bonding curve. There are no separate pools, no order books, no liquidity providers, and no counterparty matching.

Positive Forecasts push the curve up; Negative Forecasts push it down. Both directions use the same published price function, with a softplus floor that guarantees the price stays strictly above zero. The engine analyses every forecast against the full state of the book before it settles, so a single action can't leave any other position underfunded.

### How Opening and Closing Affects the NPSI

| Action | Effect on the Curve | NPSI |
|--------|---------------------|------|
| Open Positive Forecast | Curve moves up | Rises |
| Close Positive Forecast | Curve moves down | Falls |
| Open Negative Forecast | Curve moves down | Falls |
| Close Negative Forecast | Curve moves up | Rises |

### Positive Forecasts

Opening a Positive Forecast commits funds to the curve, raising the index. The user receives an internal token position sized by the curve's integral between the old and new state. Closing the position reverses the integral back down the curve and returns the proceeds — a gain if the NPSI rose while the forecast was open, a loss if it fell.

### Negative Forecasts

Opening a Negative Forecast pushes the curve down through an **escrow-backed mechanism**: the user's net stake is matched dollar-for-dollar by capital extracted from the curve, and both amounts are held in an **escrow** equal to twice the net stake. No tokens are borrowed from other users, no margin is extended, and no additional capital can ever be required from the user. Closing the forecast uses the escrow to buy the tokens back along the curve; whatever remains after the buy-back is returned to the user as their net proceeds.

- If the NPSI fell while the forecast was open, the buy-back costs less than the net stake and the user receives more than they committed.
- If the NPSI rose, the buy-back costs more than the net stake and the user receives less — possibly zero.

<Warning>
  **Liquidation.** A Negative Forecast is automatically closed if its buy-back cost reaches the liquidation threshold of its escrow. The threshold tightens at low NPSI prices to keep the book stable, and is displayed on the position before you open. When liquidation fires, the user's loss is bounded by their net stake but can equal all of it in a sharp adverse move of the NPSI.
</Warning>

### Initial Pricing

When the platform launched, the starting NPSI values for the **original 101 profiles** were set through **Elo-based ranking by beta users**: beta participants ranked profiles head-to-head until the relative ordering stabilized, and the ratings were anchored to dollar values.

For every new profile added after launch, the starting value is set through **Elo-Based Comparative Pricing**: the new profile is shown alongside two existing profiles, and participants rank where they think the new one should land — above, between, or below. Iterating thousands of times with random combinations converges on an accurate starting value.

If the starting value is slightly off at launch, the market self-corrects: forecasting activity pushes the NPSI to where the market believes it belongs.

## Fees

Every forecast, whether opening or closing, incurs a flat **1.8%** fee on the gross cash amount.

| Component | Rate | Recipient |
|-----------|------|-----------|
| Platform fee | 1.3% | Pauv |
| Good-faith royalty | 0.5% | Set aside for the public figure |
| **Total** | **1.8%** | **Split** |

- **Opening a forecast**: The 1.8% fee is deducted from the committed amount; the remaining 98.2% enters the curve as the user's net stake.
- **Closing a forecast**: The curve calculates gross proceeds; the 1.8% fee is deducted from those proceeds, and the user receives the net payout.
- **Liquidation**: If a Negative Forecast is auto-closed at the liquidation threshold, any remaining escrow after the buy-back would be reduced by the 1.8% fee before being returned.

A round trip in a quiet index (open then close with no other activity) costs approximately **3.57%**: the compounded effect of paying 1.8% on both legs.

## Sentiment Score

Alongside the headline NPSI value, Pauv exposes a **sentiment score**: the net signed dollars committed to the curve. Positive Forecasts contribute positively, Negative Forecasts contribute negatively, and the score is the integral of price over the curve's range. The sentiment score measures capital at stake, while the NPSI value measures how the index is priced.

  Forecasts on the NPSI are not securities, ownership interests, or investment contracts. They are opened and closed against a published algorithm for entertainment and signal purposes only.
</Note>
