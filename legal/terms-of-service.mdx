## 5. Trades and VBC 4.1

- **Trades.** A trade is a directional position on an NPSI expressed through Pauv's proprietary pricing engine, **VBC 4.1**, a single-curve bonding model with a softplus price floor. A **long** trade pulls the NPSI up; an escrow-backed **short** trade pulls it down. Token supply is variable and minted on the curve; there is no fixed or pre-minted per-profile inventory.
- **Short liquidation.** Shorts are fully collateralized by an on-platform escrow equal to twice the user's net stake. A short is automatically closed if the cost to buy back its tokens reaches 95% of the position's escrow; at that point, whatever remains of the escrow after the buy-back is returned to the user. A user cannot lose more than the net stake committed to a short, but may lose nearly all of it in a sharp adverse move of the NPSI.
- **No counterparty matching.** All trades settle instantly against VBC 4.1. There is no order book, no bid/ask, no secondary market, and no user-to-user matching.
- **No ownership.** Opening a trade does not give you ownership of the NPSI, the public figure, or any enterprise. The NPSI is a signal, not an asset.
- **No borrowing; no leverage.** Shorts do not borrow tokens or funds from other users. Both legs of the escrow originate from the user's net stake and capital extracted from the curve itself. Pauv does not offer margin, lending, or leveraged trades.
- **Internal positions.** VBC 4.1 uses internal token accounting, but users do not directly interact with tokens. Positions are platform records; they are not cryptocurrency, not transferable, and have no value outside the Platform.
- **Algorithmic pricing.** NPSI values are determined by trading activity through the published VBC 4.1 algorithm. A new profile's starting value is priced from public signal data (Google Trends, Instagram follower counts, and other social metrics), then anchored against existing profiles trading in a similar price range; from there the market self-corrects.
